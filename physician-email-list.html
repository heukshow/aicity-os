Buying checklist

Five checks before paying for physician contact data

1. Define the specialty and setting

Decide whether you need primary-care doctors, surgeons, specialists, private-practice physicians, hospital staff or another segment. Broad lists create waste when the offer only fits a narrow clinical audience.

2. Check verification timing

Prefer data that is checked close to export time. Bookyourdata says its email verification runs in real time before download rather than relying only on an older static file.

3. Understand the bounce policy

A deliverability claim matters more when the vendor explains how failed addresses are handled. Bookyourdata publishes a 97% deliverability guarantee and says replacement credits are provided when performance falls below that level under its guarantee terms.

4. Test a small sample

Check whether specialty, location, organization and role fields match your ideal customer profile before purchasing a larger batch. A free or low-cost sample is more useful than a large database-size claim.

5. Check the rules for your outreach

Contact-data availability does not automatically make every outreach use lawful or appropriate. Review the privacy, marketing and professional rules that apply to your country, recipients and channel before sending campaigns. This page is product-selection guidance, not legal advice.

A simple test before you scale

  1. Step 1: Build the smallest useful segment — for example one specialty, one geography and one practice type.
  2. Step 2: Export a small sample and manually inspect the fields that matter to your campaign.
  3. Step 3: Measure valid contacts and qualified responses, not just the number of records downloaded.
  4. Step 4: Buy more only if the first sample matches your targeting and outreach economics.
