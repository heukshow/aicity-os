New buyer path: Quick Sale

Need to make a sale before you build a full storefront?

Shopify's September 14 affiliate guidance highlights Quick Sale as a lower-friction way to get a merchant from product idea to payment. In the Shopify mobile app, a merchant can create a Quick Sale, add products or a custom amount, apply a discount, and collect payment without first building a full storefront.

Tap to Pay

Where supported and eligible, use a compatible iPhone or Android phone to accept contactless payment for a Quick Sale.

Payment link

Generate a secure payment-link QR code or share the link so the customer can complete payment on their own device.

Cash

Quick Sale can also record a cash payment, with the resulting order kept in Shopify rather than living in a separate sales system.

Availability matters: Quick Sale itself is available in Shopify-supported countries, but Tap to Pay has a narrower country/device eligibility list and some regions impose additional in-person retail restrictions. If Tap to Pay is unavailable, Shopify documents payment links and cash as alternative Quick Sale methods.

Shopify plan prices at a glance

The values below reflect Shopify's current US pricing page. Yearly pricing is shown as the monthly equivalent when billed annually.

Basic
$29/mo yearly · $39/mo monthly

Best starting point for a solo entrepreneur who needs the full online store, unlimited products, AI commerce tools and core reports without extra staff accounts.

Grow
$79/mo yearly · $105/mo monthly

Useful when a small team needs additional staff access and lower card rates while keeping the standard Shopify commerce stack.

Advanced
$299/mo yearly · $399/mo monthly

Designed for larger or international operations that need more staff accounts, live third-party shipping rates and regional selling controls.

Plus
From $2,300/mo

Enterprise-oriented. Consider it only when checkout customization, B2B scale, higher limits or complex multi-location operations justify the jump.

Which Shopify plan should you start with?

Start with Basic if this is your first store or you are validating demand. The lower entry price usually matters more than team features while you are still proving that traffic converts.

Move to Grow when staff access and transaction economics start to matter. Do not upgrade only because a higher tier exists.

Use Advanced when international selling, shipping-rate control or a larger operating team creates a concrete need. For most new stores, it is unnecessary at launch.

Good reasons to test Shopify

  • Hosted online store with checkout, inventory and payments in one platform.
  • Unlimited products on the core plans.
  • Built-in tools for social, marketplace and AI-channel selling.
  • Quick Sale can shorten the path from a product idea to a real transaction.
  • Trial path lets you validate the setup before standard pricing begins.

Check before paying

  • Local pricing, payment fees and promotions can differ from US pricing.
  • Apps and premium themes can increase the total monthly cost.
  • Quick Sale and Tap to Pay availability/requirements differ by location.
  • Choose a higher plan only when its operational savings exceed the price difference.
Decision point

Prove you can take a payment before you over-design the store

The useful first test is whether you can publish or add a real product and get a real customer through a payment flow. Use Quick Sale if that is the faster path for your situation, then invest in the full storefront only when the business needs it.
